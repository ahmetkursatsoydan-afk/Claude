Title: From Lung to Brain: Linking Chest CT Tumour Characteristics with Brain MRI Metastatic Patterns in Adenocarcinoma, Squamous Cell, Small Cell and Neuroendocrine Lung Cancer  

Keywords: brain metastases · lung cancer · adenocarcinoma · squamous cell · SCLC · MRI · CT  

## 1. Aims and objectives

### Aim

To illustrate how brain MRI features of metastases vary with the histological subtype of the primary lung cancer.  

### Objectives

1. Recognise the chest CT appearance of adenocarcinoma, squamous cell, small cell and large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma.
2. Describe multi-sequence MRI patterns (T1+C, T2/FLAIR, DWI) of the associated brain metastases.
3. Compare number, location, enhancement, oedema and diffusion behaviour between subtypes.
4. Identify mimics and pitfalls, e.g. an incidental meningioma alongside metastases.
5. Understand how imaging supports staging, PCI/SRS decisions and follow-up.

### Background  (scientific exhibit: keep under Aims and objectives; educational exhibit: use the separate 'Background' section)

- Lung cancer is the leading source of brain metastases (BM), about 40–50% of all BM [1,2].
- Cumulative BM risk is higher in small cell lung cancer (SCLC) than in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) [1,2].

Histology shapes risk and behaviour  

- **Adenocarcinoma:** commonest NSCLC subtype with BM; risk increased with EGFR/ALK alterations [8].
- **SCLC:** highly proliferative; frequent BM; MRI surveillance and prophylactic cranial irradiation (PCI) are debated [5,6].
- **Squamous cell carcinoma:** BM less frequent [10]; primaries often central, cavitating or necrotic.
- **LCNEC:** large cell neuroendocrine carcinoma, biologically closer to SCLC [4].
- Contrast-enhanced MRI is the most sensitive modality; lesion number, size and site decide surgery, stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) or whole-brain radiotherapy (WBRT) [6,7].

Concept: primary lung tumour (chest CT, histology-related morphology) → haematogenous spread → brain (grey–white junction, watershed, cerebellum).  

## 2. Methods and materials

- **Design:** retrospective, descriptive pictorial case series.
- **Approvals:** ethics committee; written informed consent for publication of anonymised images.
- **Cohort:** adenocarcinoma (ADC) n = 5; small cell carcinoma (SCLC) n = 3; LCNEC n = 1; squamous cell carcinoma (SCC) n = 1.
- **Illustrated:** six representative cases (ADC-2, ADC-3, SCLC-1, SCLC-2, LCNEC-1, SCC-1).
- **Inclusion:** histological diagnosis, chest CT and brain MRI available.
- **Imaging:** chest CT (axial, coronal; lung and mediastinal windows) and brain MRI: axial post-contrast T1 (T1+C), T2, FLAIR, DWI with apparent diffusion coefficient maps.
- **Analysis:** consensus review; descriptive statistics only.

Key imaging features  

- **SCLC-2** — Site: right frontoparietal; T1+C: small ring; Oedema: extensive; DWI: rim restriction.
- **SCLC-1** — Site: right paramedian frontoparietal; T1+C: small, heterogeneous; Oedema: extensive; DWI: focal restriction.
- **LCNEC-1** — Site: left frontal; T1+C: large necrotic, thick wall; Oedema: minimal; DWI: rim restriction.

Teaching points  

- MRI detects more and smaller lesions than CT: always scan the post-contrast series [5].
- Pitfall (SCLC-1): an extra-axial enhancing lesion may be an incidental meningioma, not a metastasis.
- LCNEC behaves like SCLC; imaging overlaps, so histology cannot be inferred from MRI.

## 4. Conclusion

### Discussion

- Biology shapes the imaging phenotype, but overlap is large.

**Adenocarcinoma.** Highest BM propensity among NSCLC; frequent in oncogene-driven disease [8]. Marked vasogenic oedema plausibly reflects VEGF-mediated permeability.  

**SCLC/LCNEC.** High proliferative index and early haematogenous spread [4]; SCLC often shows multiple, small lesions [11]. Contrast MRI is superior to CT for detection (staging, PCI) [5].  

**Squamous cell carcinoma.** Less frequent BM [10]; necrotic/cystic ring-enhancing lesions described.  

**This series.** In the illustrated cases, SCLC primaries were peripheral, not central, with extensive brain oedema; LCNEC was cystic with little oedema. DWI restriction was frequent (5 of 6) but does not predict histology [9].  

**Pitfalls.** Meningioma, abscess, glioma, radionecrosis, second primary.  

**Limitations.** n = 10 (6 illustrated), retrospective, heterogeneous protocols, no inferential statistics.

### Take-home messages

1. Contrast-enhanced multi-sequence MRI for lung cancer patients at staging and with new neurological symptoms [6].
2. Report lesion number, location, oedema and diffusion: they guide SRS, WBRT and surgery [3,6].
3. Adenocarcinoma: look for conspicuous oedema. SCLC/neuroendocrine: look for many small lesions.
4. No single feature is histology-specific; beware mimics (e.g. meningioma) and correlate with pathology.

Histology suggests, but does not predict, the MRI pattern.
